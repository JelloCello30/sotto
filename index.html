Type with your lips, not your fingers.

Sotto watches your mouth through your camera and types for you. Mouth a phrase it knows in total silence, or whisper anything at all — it types both, decides which is which on its own, and learns your lips while you speak, so your silent vocabulary grows on its own. Everything runs on-device in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

How it works

Three steps, no magic.

  1. 01

    Start

    One button. The camera comes on; with voice assist on (it is by default), the microphone follows and a local speech model warms up in the background. Decline the mic and Sotto carries on camera-only — silent phrases still work.

  2. 02

    Say it or mouth it

    Speak, whisper, or mouth in silence — when your lips move, Sotto cuts the utterance out of the stream and routes it on its own: a phrase it recognizes types instantly, anything audible goes to the speech model, and you never pick a mode. Audio only counts while your own mouth is moving, which shuts out background speech.

  3. 03

    It types — and learns

    Known phrases land in about 10 milliseconds; whispered words stream in as they are recognized. And every short phrase you say aloud teaches Sotto its silent shape — say it once, and next time you can just mouth it.

The pipeline, precisely: MediaPipe Face Landmarker (478 landmarks and 52 blendshapes per frame, entirely on-device) → 22 mouth features per frame → utterance segmentation by motion energy → DTW template matching against your phrase library, taught and learned alike. No lipreading neural net, no server. That is the entire trick, and it is enough. The voiced branch: the same lip segmentation clips a matching window from a local microphone buffer and hands it to a vendored Whisper model (transformers.js on ONNX Runtime — WebGPU when available, WASM otherwise). The lips gate when; the audio decides what. And when the transcript is a short, clean phrase, the audio supervises the visual templates: Sotto enrolls that utterance's lip motion under the transcribed text, on-device, so the phrase joins your silent vocabulary. Still no server.

Install

Install on any device.

Sotto is a web app that installs like a native one. No store, no installer, no account — Chrome, Edge, and Safari all know how. (Firefox can use it in a tab; it just doesn't do installing.)

  • Chrome or Edge — click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, then choose Install.
  • Safari 17 or newer — File menu, then Add to Dock.
  • Chrome or Edge — click the install icon in the address bar, then choose Install. That is the whole procedure.
  • Chromium, Chrome, or Edge — click the install icon in the address bar. The result behaves like any other desktop app, launcher entry included.
  • Open Sotto in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. It launches full-screen and asks for the camera the first time you calibrate.
  • In Chrome, open the browser menu and choose Add to Home screen — on recent versions it is labeled Install app.

You need a camera and a modern browser. After the first load, Sotto works offline.

Native apps that type system-wide are on the roadmap. They have not shipped, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Does this replace my keyboard?+

For phrases it knows, nearly: a recognized phrase types in about 10 milliseconds, in total silence if you like. For everything else there is dictation: open vocabulary, transcribed on this device in about a second per utterance once warm, with your lips gating when it listens. The honest catch is that dictation needs at least whispered speech — air moving through actual words — and the silent side covers exactly what Sotto has been taught or has heard you say. Open dictation from lip movement alone remains an unsolved research problem: the best published systems, trained on enormous curated datasets, still miss roughly a quarter of the words. From a webcam, in your kitchen lighting, it is harder still. So: if you can whisper, you can dictate — and each phrase you say aloud grows what you can later mouth in silence.

Is audio recorded?+

No — and here is the precise shape of "no". Voice assist is on by default, and while it is on the microphone is live: audio sits in a rolling in-memory buffer on this device and is transcribed by a local speech model. Nothing is recorded, stored, or uploaded — there is no server in the pipeline at all. One switch in settings turns voice assist off, and the microphone is released the instant you flip it; the browser's mic indicator turns off with it. With the mic off, Sotto is camera-only and your silent phrases keep working.

Video is the same story either way: frames are processed on-device by the face landmarker and discarded. What persists is your phrase library — a small set of motion numbers per phrase, stored in your browser.

Do I have to calibrate?+

No. Calibration is optional: with voice assist on, Sotto learns silent phrases from ordinary speech — say a short phrase aloud and it enrolls that phrase's lip motion automatically, on this device. Manual teaching remains for phrases you would rather never say out loud. What has not changed is why a vocabulary exists at all: lips underdetermine speech. Many sounds are made in the throat or with the tongue and look identical from the outside — mouth "pat", "bat", and "mat" at a mirror and watch. A personal template library turns an impossible guessing game into a tractable one: Sotto only has to decide which of your phrases you mouthed, on your face, in your light. That constraint is what makes it reliable — and it is why fully-silent-from-scratch input still means Sotto has to know the phrases first, learned from your voice or taught by hand.

It's not catching my phrases — what do I do?+

In rough order of payoff. Face the camera squarely, in decent light — the tracker reads mouth geometry, and a half-lit three-quarter profile hands it half the signal. Mouth deliberately, not minimally: the matcher compares motion against your recorded takes, and a phrase mumbled through barely-open lips does not look like the phrase you taught. Raise the sensitivity slider in settings — it lowers the bar both for cutting an utterance out of the stream and for accepting a match. Then spend a minute in practice mode and watch the near-misses: a phrase that keeps ranking just under the line wants a retake, and two phrases that keep trading places look too much alike on the lips — reword one. If it still misbehaves, flip on Diagnostics in settings and press Copy report: the report is compact status data — numbers and recent events, no audio, no video — and it is exactly the thing to paste when asking for help.

What's the roadmap?+

Bigger vocabularies with faster calibration. Phoneme-level decoding experiments, labeled as experiments. A native macOS helper that can type into any application, not just this one. In that order — and nothing gets announced here until it runs.
