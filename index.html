Type with your lips, not your fingers.

Sotto watches your mouth through your camera and types for you — calibrated phrases you mouth in silence, or open-vocabulary dictation you whisper. Both modes run on-device in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Two modes, honestly labeled. Phrase mode reads a vocabulary you teach it, in total silence, camera only. Whisper mode takes any words you can at least whisper: your lips decide when it listens, a local speech model decides what you said. Neither is silent open-vocabulary lipreading — that doesn't exist yet, here or anywhere.

How it works

Three steps, no magic. One new mode.

  1. 01

    Calibrate

    Silently mouth each phrase a few times. Sotto stores the motion pattern — a few hundred numbers — not the video. Your library lives in your browser and nowhere else.

  2. 02

    Mouth it

    When your lips start moving, Sotto notices, and cuts the utterance out of the stream on its own. No button to hold, no wake word — your lips are the signal.

  3. 03

    It types

    The closest phrase in your library lands in the pad with a confidence score attached. Turn on auto-copy and it goes straight to your clipboard.

  4. 04

    Or whisper it

    New in v0.2: Whisper mode drops the vocabulary and adds the microphone. Your lips still decide when Sotto listens — audio only counts while your own mouth is moving, which shuts out background speech — and a vendored Whisper model running on this device decides what you said. A whisper is enough; full silence is not.

The pipeline, precisely: MediaPipe Face Landmarker (478 landmarks and 52 blendshapes per frame, entirely on-device) → 22 mouth features per frame → utterance segmentation by motion energy → DTW template matching against your calibrated phrases. No lipreading neural net, no server. That is the entire trick, and it is enough. Whisper mode adds one branch: the same lip segmentation clips a matching window from a local microphone buffer and hands it to a vendored Whisper model (transformers.js on ONNX Runtime — WebGPU when available, WASM otherwise). The lips gate when; the audio decides what. Still no server.

Install

Install on any device.

Sotto is a web app that installs like a native one. No store, no installer, no account — Chrome, Edge, and Safari all know how. (Firefox can use it in a tab; it just doesn't do installing.)

  • Chrome or Edge — click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, then choose Install.
  • Safari 17 or newer — File menu, then Add to Dock.
  • Chrome or Edge — click the install icon in the address bar, then choose Install. That is the whole procedure.
  • Chromium, Chrome, or Edge — click the install icon in the address bar. The result behaves like any other desktop app, launcher entry included.
  • Open Sotto in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. It launches full-screen and asks for the camera the first time you calibrate.
  • In Chrome, open the browser menu and choose Add to Home screen — on recent versions it is labeled Install app.

You need a camera and a modern browser. After the first load, Sotto works offline.

Native apps that type system-wide are on the roadmap. They have not shipped, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Does this replace my keyboard?+

Phrase mode, no: it types phrases you have taught it — a calibrated vocabulary, fully silent, mic-free. Whisper mode gets closer: open-vocabulary dictation, transcribed on this device, with your lips gating when it listens. The honest catch is that it needs at least whispered speech — air moving through actual words. Open dictation from lip movement alone remains an unsolved research problem: the best published systems, trained on enormous curated datasets, still miss roughly a quarter of the words. From a webcam, in your kitchen lighting, it is harder still. So: if you can whisper, you can dictate. If you must stay fully silent, you have your phrases — and they work.

Is audio recorded?+

Two modes, two answers. Phrase mode: never — microphone permission is not even requested, so your browser cannot hand Sotto audio by accident. Whisper mode: the microphone is live while that mode is on, and the audio is processed on this device by a local speech model, in memory. Nothing is recorded, stored, or uploaded — there is no server in the pipeline at all. Switch back to Phrase mode and the mic turns off immediately.

Video is the same story in both modes: frames are processed on-device by the face landmarker and discarded. What persists is your phrase library — a small set of motion numbers per phrase, stored in your browser.

Why do I have to calibrate?+

Because lips underdetermine speech. Many sounds are made in the throat or with the tongue and look identical from the outside — mouth "pat", "bat", and "mat" at a mirror and watch. A personal template vocabulary turns an impossible guessing game into a tractable one: Sotto only has to decide which of your phrases you mouthed, on your face, in your light. That constraint is what makes it reliable. Calibration belongs to Phrase mode; Whisper mode skips it entirely, because it has audio to listen to.

What's the roadmap?+

Bigger vocabularies with faster calibration. Phoneme-level decoding experiments, labeled as experiments. A native macOS helper that can type into any application, not just this one. In that order — and nothing gets announced here until it runs.
